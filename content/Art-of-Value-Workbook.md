### Turn art from a decorative afterthought into a measurable driver of property value — one exercise at a time.

> A hands-on workbook for hoteliers, developers, interior designers and asset managers. In the spirit of a *For Dummies* guide: plain language, real examples, and exercises you apply straight to your own project. By Gallery Dutch Art.

## Module 1 — Why art is a business asset

For a century, hotel art was an afterthought — chosen last, to "fill the walls." The best operators now treat it as **positioning**: one of the few things a competitor can't copy. Art moves four commercial levers: **rate** (a distinctive property justifies higher prices), **dwell & spend** (guests linger and share), **earned attention** (press and social gravitate to a signature piece), and **asset value** (a curated collection strengthens the property as an asset).

⚠️ **Warning — the four budget-killers:** buying art *too late* (costly retrofits), *decorating instead of curating* (pretty but forgettable), *ignoring lighting and scale* (great art, wrong wall), and *no narrative* (unrelated pieces). Avoiding these four is most of the battle.

🧠 **Remember:** Great art turns a beautiful building into a *destination*.

✅ **Try It Yourself — The value case (10 min)**
For your property, write one concrete sentence for each lever:
```
RATE:      Art could justify a higher rate because __________
DWELL:     Guests would linger/share in ______ if we placed ______
ATTENTION: Our "you have to see it" moment could be ______
ASSET:     A curated collection would signal ______ to owners/buyers
```
Circle the lever that matters most to *your* business right now. That becomes your programme's north star.

## Module 2 — Building your art strategy

Before choosing a single piece, decide the **story**. A beach resort whispering "barefoot luxury" needs a different art language than a city hotel signalling "power and culture."

✅ **Try It Yourself — Your art narrative (10 min)**
🧰 **Template — Narrative**
```
Property: __________
3–4 adjectives: __________
The feeling on arrival: __________
One sentence: "Our art makes guests feel ______ by ______."
```

Now map the **emotional arc** as a guest experiences it, and give each zone a job.

🧰 **Template — Space-by-space plan**
```
Arrival / lobby:   role = STATEMENT   scale = ___   mood = ___
Corridors/journey: role = RHYTHM      scale = ___   mood = ___
Rooms & suites:    role = INTIMACY    scale = ___   mood = ___
F&B / social:      role = ENERGY      scale = ___   mood = ___
Spa / wellness:    role = CALM        scale = ___   mood = ___
```

Then set the money in **three tiers** *before* you fall in love with anything.

🧰 **Template — Budget tiers**
```
HERO pieces (1–3 unforgettable works):      ~40–50% of budget = € ______
SUPPORTING collection (coherent, guest areas): ~35–45% = € ______
ACCENTS (smaller pieces):                    ~10–15% = € ______
```

💡 **Tip:** As a rough hospitality benchmark, art often runs 1–3% of the FF&E budget — but the *hero* pieces deliver the disproportionate return.

## Module 3 — Sourcing & curation

Know your options and what each buys you: **originals** (max prestige/asset value, highest cost), **limited editions** (prestige at lower cost, good for consistency), **open prints** (budget accents), and **commissions** (bespoke, made for your space — the strongest tool for a hero piece). Most strong programmes mix all four by tier.

⚠️ **Warning — provenance & rights:** For every significant piece, keep authenticity/provenance documentation, and put in writing: ownership, the right to reproduce images (your marketing will want them), copyright (the artist usually keeps it), and what happens on resale or renovation.

✅ **Try It Yourself — Hero shortlist (15 min)**
🧰 **Template — Shortlist**
```
Hero opportunity #1: space = ______  type (original/edition/commission) = ______  budget = ______  why it fits the narrative = ______
Hero opportunity #2: ______
Hero opportunity #3: ______
```
Curation is *editing*: a collection feels intentional when works share a thread (palette, theme, era, medium, idea). The test — could a guest sense a point of view *without reading a label*? If yes, you've curated. If no, you've decorated.

## Module 4 — Placement, production & installation

Each zone has rules. **Lobby:** one hero, high impact, visible from the entrance. **Suites:** calmer, tactile, nothing that disturbs rest. **F&B:** characterful, durable against heat and grease. **Spa:** serene, often abstract, chosen for humidity. **Outdoor/pool:** weather-resilient sculpture.

⚠️ **Warning:** The most common waste of money is great art, badly lit or wrongly sized. Budget for proper art lighting from day one, involve the lighting designer early, and **scale boldly** — under-sized art on a big wall looks timid and cheap.

A **signature installation** (sculptural, kinetic or digital) is often the single best ROI move — the photographed, shared, "you have to see it" moment. Plan it at design stage, with early engineering and a maintenance plan.

✅ **Try It Yourself — Install readiness check**
```
[ ] Art planned at design stage (not retrofit)
[ ] Dedicated art lighting budgeted
[ ] Scale checked against the actual wall/sightline
[ ] Fixings, power & structure confirmed with the build programme
[ ] Crating, transport, customs, framing, insurance & install crew planned
```
Any unchecked box is a delay or a cost overrun waiting to happen. Fix them now.

## Module 5 — Impact, care & working with a consultant

What gets measured gets funded next time. You won't get perfect attribution — you don't need it. A handful of directional signals justifies the programme and guides the next.

🧰 **Template — KPI tracker**
| Signal | How to track | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Social tags of artworks | Search location + hashtags | Monthly |
| Press / editorial pickups | Media monitoring | Quarterly |
| Review mentions of art | Review tool keyword search | Monthly |
| Dwell & spend in art spaces | POS + observation | Quarterly |
| Sales-gallery conversion (residences) | CRM | Per phase |

Art is an asset that needs **care**: cleaning, condition checks, lighting upkeep, and a register of what you own, where it is, and its value. **Rotation** keeps repeat guests engaged — and is the foundation of an "art-as-a-service" model.

💡 **Tip — DIY vs hire:** Do it yourself when scope, budget and stakes are modest and you have taste in-house. Bring in a consultant for hero pieces, commissions, multi-asset developments, tight construction timelines, or when art must perform commercially. A good consultant pays for themselves through better sourcing, fewer mistakes and faster delivery.

✅ **Try It Yourself — Choose your path**
```
This project is: [ ] DIY  [ ] Consultant-led  [ ] Hybrid
The single riskiest part is: ______  → who owns it: ______
```

## Cheat Sheet (tear-out)
- **Art is positioning, not decoration.** It moves rate, dwell, attention and asset value.
- **Story first.** Narrative → space-by-space plan → budget tiers → pieces.
- **Spend on heroes.** 1–3 unforgettable works carry the brand.
- **Plan early.** Art at design stage; light it properly; scale boldly.
- **Protect the asset.** Provenance, image rights, care, rotation, a register.
- **The test:** a guest should sense a point of view *without reading a label.*
